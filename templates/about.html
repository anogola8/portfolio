About

How I work

I'm drawn to problems where software has to be trustworthy: records that can't quietly change, access that's properly controlled, and systems that people can actually use.

The path in

I'm a final-year Business Information Technology student at Meru University of Science and Technology, graduating 2027. My entry point was curiosity about how systems fail — not how they work when everything is fine, but what happens when the database loses a row, when a payment callback never arrives, when an officer's phone has been offline for three weeks and comes back with new records.

The problems that interest me

Trust boundaries. Offline-first records. Verifiable work history. Access that survives the second feature. These are the themes that run through my projects — not as theory, but as things I've had to code around.

The method

  1. 01

    Understand the trust boundary

    Before code, I ask: who is trusted, who is not, what breaks if the boundary shifts.

  2. 02

    Schema before screens

    Data model first — the access model can't be retrofitted cleanly.

  3. 03

    Thin vertical slices

    One complete flow end-to-end before adding the second.

  4. 04

    Test the failure modes

    Bad input fails cleanly. Rate limits, missing MFA, expired tokens.

  5. 05

    Document decisions

    Code says what. Commits and design notes say why.

The direction

I'm pursuing internships, entry-level roles, and freelance work in backend or full-stack development — particularly in security-adjacent engineering, offline-first design, and digital trust infrastructure for Kenya and the wider African market.
